## Arguments Made for Repealing Presidential Term Limits

Those who advocate for eliminating the 22nd Amendment typically present several theoretical arguments about governance and democratic choice:

### Leadership Continuity and Experience

**Institutional Knowledge**: Presidents develop extensive relationships with world leaders, deep understanding of complex policy issues, and mastery of the executive branch machinery over time. Proponents argue this accumulated expertise is lost every eight years.

**Crisis Management**: During major crises (wars, economic downturns, pandemics), changing leadership mid-course could potentially disrupt response efforts. Historical examples like FDR's four terms during the Depression and WWII are often cited.

### Democratic Choice Arguments

**Voter Sovereignty**: Some argue that term limits restrict democratic choice by preventing voters from reelecting a leader they approve of, essentially making the Constitution "vote" against the people's preferred candidate.

**Electoral Accountability**: Without term limits, presidents would theoretically remain accountable to voters throughout their tenure, rather than becoming "lame ducks" in their second term.

### Practical Governance Considerations

**Long-term Planning**: Complex initiatives like infrastructure projects, climate policy, or social reforms often require more than eight years to fully implement and see results.

**International Relations**: Some argue that frequent leadership changes can weaken America's negotiating position with countries whose leaders serve longer terms.

## Important Context

The 22nd Amendment was ratified in 1951 after FDR's unprecedented four terms. The two-term tradition existed informally since Washington until FDR. Most democracies worldwide have term limits or other checks on executive power, viewing them as essential safeguards against authoritarian drift.
