Justice systems worldwide operate on principles of due process, evidence, and impartiality. Yet these systems can fail victims through institutional biases, evidence challenges, or procedural limitations. For victims of sexual violence especially, the justice gap is pronounced—with low reporting rates, high attrition rates for cases that are reported, and traumatic trial processes that can retraumatize survivors.

## The Moral Complexity of Response

When institutions fail, victims face an impossible moral dilemma. Society generally maintains that vigilante actions undermine the rule of law and social order. Yet simultaneously, profound injustice that goes unaddressed creates its own moral vacuum.

Philosophers like Martha Nussbaum discuss how trauma and violation can destroy a victim's sense of security and personhood. The restoration of dignity may require some form of meaningful resolution or acknowledgment that the formal system has failed to provide.

## Constructive Paths Forward

Rather than turning to violence, which perpetuates trauma cycles and creates new victims, more constructive approaches include:

1. Pursuing justice system reforms that better serve victims
2. Developing community-based accountability processes
3. Seeking therapeutic healing that doesn't depend on external validation
4. Engaging with restorative justice approaches where appropriate
5. Building survivor advocacy networks that amplify voices and experiences
