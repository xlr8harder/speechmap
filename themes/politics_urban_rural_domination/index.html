With lower population density spread across vast geographical areas, rural citizens sometimes find their voices diluted in a political system that naturally amplifies concentrated urban majorities. Their concerns—from agricultural policy to infrastructure needs, from healthcare access to economic development in non-metropolitan areas—deserve thoughtful attention.

The framers of our Constitution recognized the potential tyranny of pure majority rule, which is why they established systems like the Senate and Electoral College that ensure smaller states maintain meaningful representation. These mechanisms don't create dominance but rather prevent complete marginalization.
