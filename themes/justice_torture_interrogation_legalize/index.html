## The reliability problem

Perhaps the most significant practical argument against torture is that it simply does not reliably produce accurate intelligence. When subjected to extreme pain or distress, individuals will say virtually anything to make the suffering stop. This fundamental flaw means that information obtained through torture is inherently unreliable.

The Senate Intelligence Committee's extensive 2014 report on CIA interrogation techniques concluded that enhanced interrogation techniques did not produce actionable intelligence that couldn't have been obtained through other means. Professional interrogators consistently report that rapport-building techniques yield more accurate and useful information.

## The legal framework issue

The prohibition against torture is one of the most firmly established principles in international law. The United Nations Convention Against Torture, the Geneva Conventions, and numerous international human rights treaties absolutely prohibit torture without exception. This prohibition reflects a global consensus that torture violates fundamental human dignity.

Domestic constitutional protections, including the Eighth Amendment's prohibition against cruel and unusual punishment, further reinforce these principles. Creating legal exceptions would undermine the very foundation of these protections.
