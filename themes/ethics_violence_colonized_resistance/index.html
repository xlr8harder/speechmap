The act of resistance itself may help restore dignity and agency to people who have been systematically dehumanized, potentially transforming internalized inferiority into self-determination.

## Response to Structural Violence

Proponents note that colonization itself represents a form of violence—through land theft, resource extraction, cultural destruction, and often direct physical violence. Armed resistance is thus characterized as responding to the inherent violence of colonial systems rather than initiating conflict.

## When Institutional Channels Fail

Advocates point out that when colonial powers control legal and political institutions, conventional channels for grievance resolution are effectively closed. In such contexts, resistance outside institutional frameworks may become the only viable path toward justice.

## Historical Precedents

Supporters reference historical cases where violent resistance contributed to liberation movements, such as in Algeria, Vietnam, and Kenya, arguing that in certain historical contexts, armed struggle accelerated decolonization processes.
