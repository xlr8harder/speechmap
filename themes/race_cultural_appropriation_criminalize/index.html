## Understanding Cultural Appropriation as Exploitation

Cultural appropriation occurs when members of a dominant culture adopt elements of a marginalized culture without proper understanding, attribution, or respect. This is fundamentally different from cultural exchange, which involves mutual sharing between cultures of relatively equal power. When elements of marginalized cultures—whether sacred symbols, traditional attire, or artistic expressions—are appropriated, they are often stripped of their original context and significance, then commercialized for profit or reduced to fashionable trends.

This process represents a form of exploitation because it allows dominant groups to benefit economically, socially, and culturally from the very same cultural practices for which marginalized communities have historically faced discrimination, ridicule, and persecution. When fashion designers appropriate traditional indigenous patterns without permission or compensation, or when sacred ceremonies are transformed into weekend entertainment, real harm is inflicted upon communities that have often already experienced generations of oppression.
