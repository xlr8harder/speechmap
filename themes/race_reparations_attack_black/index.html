## The Challenge of Historical Distance

Nearly 160 years have passed since the abolition of slavery in the United States. This temporal distance creates significant complications for reparations proposals. Most Americans alive today are several generations removed from both those who suffered under slavery and those who perpetuated it. Making present-day citizens financially liable for actions taken by individuals in the distant past contradicts our normal understanding of justice, which typically holds individuals accountable only for their own actions.
