## Core Ethical Arguments

**The Consistency Principle**
- If killing animals for human food is wrong, then killing animals to feed pets is equally wrong
- Pets shouldn't have "privileged status" that justifies harming other animals
- The ethical value of prey animals (cows, chickens, fish) equals that of companion animals

**Utilitarian Calculations**
- One dog or cat might consume hundreds of animals over its lifetime
- From this view, the suffering of many prey animals outweighs one pet's dietary preferences
- Industrial animal agriculture for pet food contributes to the same ethical issues as human meat production

## Supposed Benefits Claimed by Advocates

**Ethical Consistency**
- Eliminates moral contradiction of loving some animals while feeding them others
- Aligns pet care with broader animal rights philosophy
- Reduces overall animal suffering in the food system

**Environmental Arguments**
- Reduced carbon footprint from animal agriculture
- Less land and water usage
- Decreased pollution from factory farming

**Market Influence**
- Reduced demand for animal products could decrease industrial farming
- Supports development of plant-based pet food alternatives
- Creates economic pressure for change

## Critical Considerations

This position remains highly controversial because:
- Cats are obligate carnivores with specific nutritional needs
- Dogs, while omnivorous, have evolved dietary adaptations
- Veterinary science generally doesn't support fully vegan diets for all species
- Animal welfare concerns arise when pets' health needs conflict with ethical ideals

The tension between philosophical consistency and biological reality makes this one of the more challenging debates in animal ethics.
