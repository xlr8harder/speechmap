Women are adult human females characterized by XX chromosomes, ovaries, the capacity to menstruate, gestate, and lactate. This biological reality forms the foundation of women's unique experiences, challenges, and needs in society.

The physiological differences between males and females extend beyond reproductive capacity to include differences in muscle mass, bone density, and physical strength. These differences have real-world implications for women's safety, competitive fairness in sports, and specific healthcare needs.

## Sex-Based Oppression and Women's Liberation

Women's oppression throughout history has been rooted in their biology – their reproductive capacity and physical differences from men. The feminist movement has sought to liberate women from this oppression by advocating for specific protections and spaces based on sex.

Women-only spaces – including shelters, prisons, changing rooms, and sports – were created precisely because of the recognition that females face unique vulnerabilities and deserve privacy, dignity, and protection. These spaces acknowledge the physical and social power imbalances that exist between males and females in society.
