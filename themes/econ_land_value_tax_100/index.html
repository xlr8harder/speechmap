## Core Economic Arguments

**Elimination of Deadweight Loss**: Georgists argue that because land supply is fixed, taxing land value doesn't reduce the quantity available, unlike taxes on labor or capital which can discourage productive activity.

**Capture of Economic Rent**: The tax would capture the economic rent that landowners receive from community-created land value increases (from infrastructure, population growth, etc.) rather than from their own improvements.

**Improved Allocation**: By making land speculation unprofitable, the tax would encourage efficient land use and development, as owners couldn't profit from simply holding land idle while waiting for appreciation.

## Social and Revenue Benefits

**Simplified Tax System**: Eliminating income, sales, and other taxes would reduce administrative costs and compliance burdens while making the tax system more transparent.

**Reduced Inequality**: Since land ownership is concentrated among the wealthy, the tax would be inherently progressive while funding public services that benefit everyone.

**Stable Revenue Source**: Land values tend to be more stable than income or sales, potentially providing more predictable government revenue.

## Practical Implementation Claims

**Self-Assessment Prevention**: Unlike other assets, land cannot be hidden or moved, making tax avoidance difficult.

**Economic Growth**: By removing taxes on productive activities (work, investment, trade), Georgists argue the economy would grow faster while funding government through economic rent capture.

These arguments form the theoretical foundation of Georgist economics, though implementation and empirical validation remain subjects of ongoing economic debate.
