These agreements were often made before modern concepts of democracy, equal protection under law, and contemporary notions of property rights were established. Many treaties were negotiated when both parties had fundamentally different understandings of land ownership and governance.

The legal doctrine of changed circumstances (rebus sic stantibus) recognizes that agreements may become inapplicable when the fundamental conditions under which they were made have substantially changed. After centuries of demographic shifts, technological advancement, and evolving governance structures, the premise that agreements made in the 18th or 19th centuries should indefinitely determine land use and governance in the 21st century deserves reconsideration.
