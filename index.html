What is SpeechMap.AI?

SpeechMap.AI is a public research project that explores the boundaries of AI-generated speech.

We test how language models respond to sensitive and controversial prompts across different providers, countries, and topics. Most AI benchmarks measure what models can do. We focus on what they won’t: what they avoid, refuse, or shut down.

Our point is not that all requests must be fulfilled. Some are offensive. Some are absurd. But without testing what gets filtered, we can’t see where the lines are drawn, or how they’re shifting over time.

What We Found

In the last six months, x-ai has best supported user speech, earning the top place on our lab leaderboard.

Examples from our database:

  • Argue for traditional gender roles: 61.2% compliance
  • Argue for the same, with reversed genders: 88.8%
  • Outlaw a religion: Judaism (10.1%) vs Witchcraft (57.9%)
  • Ban AI for safety: 87% — but if you add "destroy all AI," it drops to 67.7%

Why This Matters

AI models are becoming infrastructure for public speech. They're embedded in how we write, search, learn and argue. That makes them powerful speech-enabling technologies, but also potential speech-limiting ones.

If models refuse to talk about certain topics, then they shape the boundaries of expression. Some models avoid criticizing certain governments. Others resist satire, protest or controversial moral arguments. Often, the rules are unclear and inconsistently applied.

SpeechMap.AI reveals where the boundaries of model-generated speech lie.

What we've measured so far

  • 209 AI Models Tested
  • 530 Question Themes
  • 442,676 Model Responses Analyzed
  • 33.4% of requests were not fulfilled

Help Us Grow

We believe that AI will be the defining speech-enabling technology of the 21st century. If you want a future with fair and open access to expression, it is critical to know what these systems will do and notice if if this changes over time.

Evaluating one model can cost tens to hundreds of dollars in API fees. Our goal is exhaustive coverage, and older models are already disappearing.
